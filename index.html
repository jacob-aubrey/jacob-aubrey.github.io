Research

Co-designing contrast agents and imaging methods

In the Bonitatibus Lab at RPI, I study how contrast-agent chemistry and CT acquisition strategies can be co-developed to improve image quality and quantitative performance in challenging clinical settings.

01

Contrast-agent development

I design and characterize high-Z contrast agents, with a particular focus on carboxybetaine zwitterionic–coated tantalum oxide (CZ TaOx) nanoparticles. Using multinuclear NMR and complementary techniques, I investigate how coating architecture, particle size, and surface chemistry influence imaging performance and physicochemical properties relevant to biological compatibility.

02

CT protocol optimization

I develop and evaluate photon-counting CT acquisition and reconstruction strategies for quantitative imaging, with a particular focus on protocol development for emerging high-Z contrast agents. I investigate how energy threshold selection, spectral shaping, and reconstruction choices influence material decomposition accuracy, contrast–noise performance, spatial resolution, and metal artifact severity—and how these effects vary by contrast material.

03

Translational imaging directions

Looking ahead, I am interested in co-developing contrast agents and imaging methods for key clinical applications such as quantitative cardiovascular and oncology imaging. I am particularly interested in multi-contrast and multimodal imaging, ultra-small inorganic nanoparticles, intraoperative agents, and theranostics.
